### 1b. What does NOT reproduce

4 of 32 correlation cells differ from the paper by more than 0.30.

| Measure | Model | Era | Ours | Paper | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| assortativity | conf | pre | +0.573 | -0.470 | +1.043 |
| modularity | conf | pre | +0.555 | +0.070 | +0.485 |
| assortativity | comm | pre | +0.283 | +0.760 | -0.477 |
| betweenness | comm | pre | +0.619 | +0.930 | -0.311 |

These are concentrated in the **configuration model**, which in our hands tracks the real networks far more closely than in the paper's: mean ρ **0.861** against the paper's **0.721** across all 16 configuration cells, and higher on every single measure.

Two candidate explanations were tested on a 500-day pre-2002 sample and **both were ruled out**:

- *Insufficient randomisation.* Rewiring at 5x and 50x the edge count gives identical results (ρ 0.510 vs 0.512; edge overlap with the real graph 26.4% in both). The rewiring is converged.
- *Generator choice.* Classical stub matching, the other reading of "configuration model", gives ρ_modularity +0.443 and ρ_assortativity +0.418 — closer to ours than to the paper's +0.07 and −0.47.

So two independent implementations of the configuration model both land near ρ ≈ +0.44 to +0.55 where the paper reports +0.07 and −0.47. The gap is **not explained** by anything we can identify in our pipeline. It matters because the configuration model is the paper's *yardstick*: the community model looks impressive largely in proportion to how badly the degree null performs. [analysis]

## 3. Problems with the original analysis

These are not dataset differences. They would apply to the paper's own data.

| # | Finding | Number |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Every reported correlation is computed on 30-day windows slid by one day, so consecutive points share 29/30 of their data. Recomputed on non-overlapping windows | mean ρ 0.783 → **0.777**; falls on 12/32 cells |
| **A4/B6** | The modularity excess over the degree-sequence null is reproduced by i.i.d. noise through the same pipeline | noise excess +0.0921 vs real +0.1150 = **80%** |
| **B1/B2** | Louvain is stochastic and run once per day; the seed-to-seed spread rivals the day-to-day movement being interpreted | NMI same-day 0.543 vs consecutive-day 0.463 |
| **D1/B5** | The compression claim inverts during crises, when the network shatters into singleton communities | cheaper on 69% of days but **34%** of crisis days |

| **E2** | Squared differences spanning 0.0003–2,522 printed in one table as if comparable | normalized MSE reported alongside every cell |
