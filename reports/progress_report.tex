\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Three papers on learning network structure from financial time series have been
replicated from scratch over the period 5~July~--~10~August~2026. This document
(i)~records what was built, what reproduced and what did not, in chronological
order; (ii)~extracts the findings that survived adversarial re-audit and are
therefore usable as the foundation of new work; (iii)~reviews the literature on
spectral indicators of market stress, separating the \emph{Laplacian} family
(algebraic connectivity, spectral gap, Laplacian energy) from the
\emph{modularity-matrix} family (leading and trailing eigenvalues, structural
eigenvalue counts, detectability thresholds); and (iv)~proposes three research
directions, with preliminary numerical evidence computed on the 6{,}315-day
network sequence already built for Paper~3. The headline of that preliminary
evidence: on the same data, under the same scoring protocol, the scalar
modularity $Q$ detects crises at ROC-AUC $0.538$ while the spectrum of the
modularity matrix it is derived from reaches $0.760$, and the correlation
spectrum reaches $0.822$. The Fiedler value and the leading modularity
eigenvalue --- exact affine duals on a regular graph --- are empirically
\emph{near-orthogonal} on these networks ($r = -0.17$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}
% =====================================================================

The work so far has been a \emph{literature review conducted by replication}:
rather than reading three papers and summarising them, each was rebuilt from
first principles, run on real data, and audited. That was deliberate. A
summary tells you what a paper claims; a replication tells you which of those
claims survive contact with data, and --- more useful for choosing a research
problem --- which of them were never actually tested by the original authors.

The output of that phase is a set of concrete, defensible gaps. This document
converts them into a problem statement. The specific question on the table,
raised in our last discussion, is:

\begin{quote}
Paper~1 uses the \emph{Fiedler value} $\lambda_2(\Lap)$ --- the second-smallest
eigenvalue of a learned graph Laplacian --- as a market-stress indicator.
Paper~3 uses \emph{modularity} $Q$ --- a scalar derived from a partition of a
thresholded correlation network. Modularity has an underlying matrix, the
modularity matrix $\Bmat$, whose eigenvalues and singular values nobody in this
literature reports. Is there a study in comparing the Laplacian and the
modularity matrix as sources of market indicators?
\end{quote}

Section~\ref{sec:problem} argues that there is, that it is well-posed, and that
the comparison is more interesting than it first looks because the two operators
are \emph{provably the same object on a regular graph} and yet behave completely
differently on real market networks. Section~\ref{sec:directions} proposes three
directions. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} gives the preliminary numbers that motivate
them, computed this week on data already in hand.

\section{Part I --- What was built and what it showed}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Paper 1: learning graph Laplacians from returns}

\textbf{Source.} Cardoso \& Palomar, \emph{Learning Undirected Graphs in
Financial Markets}, 54th Asilomar Conference on Signals, Systems and Computers,
2020, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09958}{arXiv:2005.09958}. The extended
treatment is \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15410}{arXiv:2012.15410}.

\textbf{Method.} A penalised maximum-likelihood estimate of a precision matrix
constrained to be a valid graph Laplacian:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\Lap} \; \operatorname{tr}(\Lap S) - \log \operatorname{gdet}(\Lap) + h_\alpha(\Lap)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \Lap \mathbf{1} = 0,\; \Lap_{ij}\le 0 \;(i \ne j),\; \Lap \succeq 0,
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the sample correlation matrix and $\operatorname{gdet}$ is the
pseudo-determinant (a valid Laplacian is singular, so $\det \Lap = 0$). The
constraints have financial content: $\Lap\mathbf{1}=0$ removes the market
factor, and $\Lap_{ij}\le 0$ forces all conditional correlations non-negative.
Algorithm~1 adds a spectral penalty to force $k$ connected components;
Algorithm~2 rolls Algorithm~1 causally over 30-day windows with a temporal
smoothness penalty $\delta\sum_t \|\Lap_t - \Lap_{t-1}\|_F^2$. The indicator is
$\lambda_2(\Lap_t)$, the algebraic connectivity or Fiedler value
\cite{fiedler1973}, and the trading rule is: hold the portfolio when
$\lambda_2 < \tau$, sit in cash otherwise, with $\tau=1.0$.

\textbf{Implementation.} Rather than the paper's ADMM, the solver works in
edge-weight space: $\Lap = \mathcal{L}w$ with $w \ge 0$, which makes the
Laplacian constraints automatic and turns the problem into a bound-constrained
smooth minimisation solved by L-BFGS-B. Degree control ($\operatorname{diag}\Lap
= 1$) becomes a quadratic penalty; the $k$-component constraint becomes Fan's
spectral penalty with strength $\eta$.

\textbf{Status.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Fig.~1 (preprocessing) --- \ok. Panel (d), scaled data with the market
factor \emph{not} explicitly removed, is cleanest, as the paper claims.
\item Fig.~2 ($k$-component vs.\ SGL) --- \ok. True $k=3$ (rank $94 = p-3$ at
$p=97$) achieved, but only at $\eta = 300$; $\eta \in \{10, 150\}$ is
insufficient. The paper does not report needing this.
\item Fig.~3 (time-varying $\lambda_2$) --- \ok. Our $\lambda_2 \in [0.66,
1.50]$ matches the paper's monthly regime structure.
\item Fig.~4 (trading) --- \partialx. \emph{The mechanism replicates and the
P\&L ranking does not.} With the paper's own $\tau = 1.0$ and evaluation window
(ending 2020-05-01), the gate is live (79 of 201 days invested). S2 dodges the
COVID crash exactly as the paper shows --- flat at $\approx -0.01$ while
buy-and-hold falls to $-0.21$ --- and also sits out autumn 2019. But our
$\lambda_2$ stays near $1.3$ through April where the paper's drops below $1.0$,
so S2 misses the rebound: final S2 $= -0.011$ against S1 $= +0.078$.
No threshold was tuned to fix this.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The most instructive failure.} An early version of the temporal penalty
used $S_{\text{aug}} = S_t + (2\delta/n_t)\Lap_{t-1}$. The Frobenius cross-term
is $-2\delta\operatorname{tr}(\Lap\,\Lap_{t-1})$, so the sign was inverted: the
penalty \emph{repelled} each window from its predecessor. The symptom was
$\lambda_2$ collapsing into $[0, 0.78]$ with 65 windows at exactly zero
(disconnected estimates), which made $\tau=1.0$ non-binding and the trading
figure vacuous. This is recorded because it is the kind of error that produces a
plausible-looking figure: a stress indicator that spends its time near zero and
spikes occasionally still \emph{looks} like a stress indicator.
\jd{Worth keeping in mind for any new indicator we build: check that the
proposed threshold actually gates before interpreting the backtest.}

\subsection{Paper 2: TICC}

\textbf{Source.} Hallac, Vare, Boyd \& Leskovec, \emph{Toeplitz Inverse
Covariance-Based Clustering of Multivariate Time Series Data}, KDD 2017,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03161}{arXiv:1706.03161} \cite{hallac2017}.

\textbf{Method.} Simultaneous segmentation and clustering: each cluster is a
block-Toeplitz Gaussian MRF over a length-$w$ window; the M-step is a Toeplitz
graphical lasso solved by ADMM, the E-step an exact Viterbi assignment with a
switching penalty $\beta$.

\textbf{Status.} The headline reproduces --- TICC reaches macro-F1 $\approx
0.99$ against the paper's $0.95$ average, and is genuinely robust across data
seeds ($\sigma \le 0.006$). Three things do not:
\begin{itemize}
\item The \textbf{$\beta = 0$ ablation is systematically too weak} (our $0.80$
average vs.\ the paper's $0.88$), which means our replication \emph{overstates}
the value of the temporal-consistency penalty. This is the one place our error
points away from the paper's own conclusion, so it leads the report. \nope
\item Network recovery (Table 2) sits at $0.80$ against the paper's $0.85$ band.
\partialx
\item Fig.~4 is a timing proxy that never calls the fitting routine, over a
narrower range of $T$ than the paper's $10^4$--$10^7$. \partialx
\end{itemize}
Known thumbs on the scale, disclosed: segment length 400 everywhere instead of
the paper's $100K$ (up to $2\times$ the data), and generator edge weights bounded
away from zero. Window-size robustness, the micro-F1 cross-check and BIC
selection of $K$ are \textbf{not implemented}.

\textbf{Relevance going forward.} TICC is the one of the three that estimates a
\emph{regime}, not a snapshot. It is not central to the Laplacian-vs-modularity
question, but it is the natural comparator if we ever want to ask whether a
spectral indicator is doing anything a proper regime-switching model would not.

\subsection{Paper 3: modular dynamics of financial market networks}

\textbf{Source.} Silva, Comin, Peron, Rodrigues, Ye, Wilson, Hancock \& Costa,
\emph{Modular Dynamics of Financial Market Networks},
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.05040}{arXiv:1501.05040} (2015) \cite{silva2015}.

\textbf{Method.} Log returns; Pearson correlation over a $\Delta t = 30$-return
sliding window shifted by one day; a threshold $\tau(t)$ re-solved every day so
that exactly $f = 10\%$ of pairs survive (fixed density, not fixed threshold);
binarise. Louvain communities each day, giving $Q(t)$ and $k(t)$. Then the
central construction: keep \emph{only} the community sizes and the
inter-community mixing matrix $\Pi(t)$, discard the network, and regenerate a
random graph from that summary alone. The claim is that this compressed
description reproduces the real network's topology, and that crises move the
market from well-defined communities to a uniform organisation.

\textbf{Scale of the replication.} $N = 360$ NYSE stocks $\times$ $T = 6{,}345$
trading days (paper: $348 \times 6{,}008$), 6{,}315 daily networks, 126{,}300
generated null graphs, 132{,}615 graph evaluations across eight topological
measures. Organised around a 26-item methodological audit written \emph{before}
implementation began, with crisis windows pre-registered in Phase 0 so that no
detector could be tuned after seeing the data.

\textbf{What reproduces.} The community null tracks real modularity at $\rho =
0.98$ (paper: $0.99$). Mean degree is exactly $f(N-1) = 35.90$ every day.
Crises shatter the network: $Q$ falls to $0.0897$ on 1987-10-19, the 25-year
low, with $k \to 156$. The configuration-model noise floor is $Q = 0.1065 \pm
0.0145$, matching the $\approx\!0.10$ the paper draws but never states. Across
all 32 cells of the paper's correlation table the mean absolute difference is
$0.155$ and the sign agrees on 97\%.

\textbf{What the paper's own construction does not support.} These are the
results worth carrying forward, and they are the reason the current problem
statement exists.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The level of $Q$ is not evidence.} Running the identical pipeline
on i.i.d.\ Gaussian returns of the same shape gives Louvain $Q = 0.2246 \pm
0.0067$ against the real market's $0.2215 \pm 0.0413$. Structureless data is, if
anything, marginally \emph{more} modular. Only the \emph{variance} of $Q$
distinguishes the market ($6\times$ that of noise). The mechanism is that a
correlation matrix is positive semi-definite, so thresholding it yields a graph
that is transitive by construction, and transitive graphs are modular; no
degree-sequence null can control for this, because rewiring destroys the
geometry along with the structure.
\item \textbf{The compression claim inverts exactly where the story lives.} The
community summary costs $k + k(k+1)/2$ numbers against the degree sequence's
$N$; break-even is $k = 25.4$. It is cheaper on 69.3\% of all days but only
34.3\% of pre-registered sharp-crisis days, because the network shatters into
singletons and $k$ reaches 156.
\item \textbf{$\tau(t)$, which fixed-density thresholding throws away, detects
crises far better than modularity does.} Against the pre-registered windows
(2.7\% base rate): mean correlation $0.807$, $\tau(t)$ $0.807$, isolated-node
count $0.760$, dynamical modularity $\mathbf{0.538}$. The paper's Fig.~1 has a
box labelled ``crisis detection evaluation'' and the paper performs none; when
it is built, the paper's central quantity is the weakest of the four signals its
own pipeline produces.
\item \textbf{Louvain instability rivals the signal.} Ten seeds per day: the
same-day seed-to-seed spread in $Q$ is $2.09\times$ the median day-to-day move
and exceeds it on 77.9\% of days. Same-day NMI between seeds is $0.543$;
consecutive-day NMI at fixed seed is $0.463$. Two runs on identical data agree
only $+0.079$ more than runs on different days.
\item \textbf{The eight measures are about three.} Mean $|\rho|$ between
measures $0.607$; three of eight principal components carry 90\% of the variance.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The negative result, reported as prominently as the positive ones.} The
audit's own central hypothesis was that every correlation in the paper is
computed on 30-day windows slid by one day, so consecutive points share 29/30 of
their data, and $\approx 200$ effectively independent points are presented as
5{,}978. Recomputing on non-overlapping windows moves the mean $\rho$ from
$0.783$ to $\mathbf{0.777}$. The mechanism is real --- a unit test shows two
independent random walks correlating at $|\rho| > 0.3$ through the same windows
--- but overlap inflates correlations that are \emph{spurious}, and these are
not. Effective sample size falls from 6{,}315 to 211, so the confidence
intervals should widen; the point estimates stand.

\textbf{The re-audit (2026-08-10).} The replication was then attacked in the
same way it had attacked the paper. Results: all 32 paper cells were transcribed
correctly, and the four non-reproduced cells survive nine implementation
variants --- they are real. But the \emph{cause} is upstream of the null model:
our networks fragment on 63\% of days (99.3\% post-2002, median 30 isolated
stocks) where the paper implies fragmentation is a Black-Monday-only event.
Degree-null modularity correlates $-0.875$ with the isolated-node count, and on
fully connected days $\rho(\Q_{\text{real}}, \Q_{\text{conf}})$ falls from
$0.579$ to $0.187$, near the paper's $+0.07$. Eight defects were found in our
own criticisms (matching-index definition, a circular rich-club normalisation,
an over-stated NMSE claim, a noise-excess ratio computed against two different
floors, among others). Unaffected and solid: the compression inversion, the
Louvain instability, the overlap negative result, the noise-level control, and
the crisis detector.

\textbf{Deliberately out of scope, recorded so nothing is silently dropped:}
robustness to $\Delta t \in \{20,60,120\}$; market-mode removal before network
construction; a mechanism for the paper's own unexplained 2002 structural break.
All three re-enter as first-class questions in Section~\ref{sec:directions}.

\section{Part II --- What the three replications jointly establish}
% =====================================================================

Stripped of the paper-by-paper detail, five things are now known well enough to
build on:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{F\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Thresholding a correlation matrix manufactures the structure it
then measures.} A PSD matrix thresholded at fixed density is transitive by
construction; i.i.d.\ noise reproduces 80\% of the market's modularity excess
over a degree-sequence null. This is not specific to Paper~3 --- it applies to
any paper in the thresholded-correlation-network literature, which is precisely
the argument of the recent \emph{Physics Reports} review \cite{masuda2025}.
\item \textbf{Fixed-density thresholding discards the strongest crisis signal
the pipeline produces.} $\tau(t)$ and mean correlation both reach AUC $0.807$;
they are available before any community is detected, at zero cost.
\item \textbf{Scalar summaries of a partition are noisy and weak.} Louvain's
seed-to-seed jitter is twice the daily signal, and $Q$'s crisis-detection AUC is
$0.538$ against a 2.7\% base rate.
\item \textbf{The Laplacian-based indicator of Paper~1 works through a specific
and fragile mechanism.} $\lambda_2$ is only interpretable when the graph is
connected and the estimation is regularised; both the sign bug and the
disconnection episodes reduced it to noise.
\item \textbf{The two literatures do not talk to each other.} Paper~1 estimates
a Laplacian and reads its second-smallest eigenvalue. Paper~3 thresholds a
correlation matrix and reads a scalar derived from a partition of it. Neither
looks at the operator the other uses; neither reports a spectrum.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Part III --- Problem statement: Laplacian versus modularity spectra}
\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The two operators}

Let $\Amat$ be the adjacency matrix of a network on $N$ nodes with degrees $d_i$
and $m$ edges, $2m = \sum_i d_i$.

\begin{align}
\text{Laplacian:} \qquad & \Lap = \mathbf{D} - \Amat,
&& 0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \le \lambda_N, \\
\text{Modularity matrix:} \qquad & \Bmat = \Amat - \frac{\mathbf{d}\mathbf{d}^{\!\top}}{2m},
&& \mu_N \le \dots \le \mu_2 \le \mu_1 .
\end{align}

They are read from opposite ends. $\Lap$ is positive semi-definite; its small
eigenvalues describe how close the graph is to falling apart, and $\lambda_2$ is
the Fiedler value \cite{fiedler1973}. $\Bmat$ is indefinite by construction
($\Bmat\mathbf{1} = 0$, so it always has a zero eigenvalue with $\operatorname{tr}\Bmat < 0$);
its \emph{largest} eigenvalues describe community structure \cite{newman2006pre,
newman2006pnas}, and --- the part almost nobody uses --- its \emph{most negative}
eigenvalues describe \emph{anti}-community structure, groups that avoid each
other \cite{fasino2018anti}.

\subsection{The identity that makes the comparison non-trivial}

For a connected \emph{$d$-regular} graph, $\Lap = d\mathbf{I} - \Amat$ and
$\Bmat = \Amat - (d/N)\mathbf{J}$. The all-ones vector is the top eigenvector of
$\Amat$ and is annihilated by both, so with $\nu_i$ the adjacency eigenvalues,
\begin{equation}
\lambda_2(\Lap) = d - \nu_2(\Amat), \qquad
\mu_1(\Bmat) = \nu_2(\Amat)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\;\lambda_2(\Lap) + \mu_1(\Bmat) = d\;}
\label{eq:duality}
\end{equation}
whenever $\nu_2 > 0$. \emph{On a regular graph the Fiedler value and the leading
modularity eigenvalue are the same number, read backwards.} A study that
compared them there would be measuring nothing.

This is exactly why the comparison \emph{is} interesting on market networks.
Paper~3's construction holds the \emph{mean} degree at $f(N-1) = 35.90$ on every
single day, so the networks are as close to the regular case as any real
construction gets --- and yet, measured on all 6{,}315 days:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & Value \\
\midrule
$\bar d$ (exact, every day) & $35.900$ \\
Residual $\bar d - [\lambda_2(\Lap) + \mu_1(\Bmat)]$ & $9.49 \pm 4.63$ \\
\quad restricted to the 2{,}342 connected days & $9.21 \pm 3.60$ \\
\quad on the largest connected component only & $11.11 \pm 5.31$ \\
$\operatorname{corr}(\lambda_2(\Lap), \mu_1(\Bmat))$ & $\mathbf{-0.187}$ \\
\quad connected days only & $-0.119$ \\
\quad largest connected component only & $-0.169$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The identity fails by 26\% of the mean degree, and the two quantities are
\emph{near-orthogonal in practice}. That gap is not noise: it is degree
heterogeneity and fragmentation, and it is measurable. \textbf{The research
question is therefore not ``which indicator is better'' but ``the two operators
are duals in theory and independent in practice --- what exactly is the
difference measuring, and does it carry information about market stress?''}
That is a well-posed question with a clean answer available.
\pc{This framing is the part I would most like your reaction to. The alternative
framing --- a plain empirical horse race between indicators --- is easier to
execute but much less interesting, and reviewers in this area have seen many
horse races.}

\subsection{Literature map}

\paragraph{Laplacian-side indicators in finance.} Cardoso \& Palomar
\cite{cardoso2020,cardoso2021} learn a Laplacian-constrained precision matrix and
use $\lambda_2$ as the stress indicator; this is Paper~1. Kang, Yen \& Cheong
\cite{kang2025} build filtration networks from correlation distances and use the
\emph{maximum spectral gap} of the unnormalised Laplacian,
$\max_{k\ge 2}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_k)$, arguing that it is large during
crashes and zero otherwise, and using it to assign crashes a \emph{duration}
rather than a date. A parallel strand uses aggregate spectral functionals rather
than single eigenvalues: Laplacian energy \cite{lapenergy2023} and the
Laplacian-energy-like measure \cite{lel2025} as early-warning inputs. On the
covariance side rather than the graph side, the absorption ratio of Kritzman,
Li, Page \& Rigobon \cite{kritzman2011} --- the variance share of the leading
principal components --- is the best-established spectral systemic-risk measure
in finance, and is a mandatory baseline for anything we propose.

\paragraph{Modularity-side theory.} Newman introduced the modularity matrix and
the leading-eigenvector method \cite{newman2006pre,newman2006pnas}. Its spectral
theory has since been worked out carefully: Bolla et al.\ \cite{bolla2015} give
conditions under which the modularity spectrum has at least one positive
eigenvalue, i.e.\ a non-trivial community structure exists at all; Fasino \&
Tudisco \cite{fasino2014,fasino2016} analyse generalised modularity matrices and
show that the number of positive eigenvalues upper-bounds the number of disjoint
modules, and \cite{fasino2018anti} use the extreme \emph{negative} eigenvalues
for anti-community detection. On the random-matrix side, Chauhan, Girvan \& Ott
\cite{chauhan2009} and Sarkar, Henderson \& Robinson \cite{sarkar2013} show that
the number of communities and the depth of a hierarchy are fingerprinted by
eigenvalues separated from the bulk; Peixoto \cite{peixoto2013} computes the
spectra of modular networks and identifies the point at which the isolated
eigenvalues merge into the bulk; Nadakuditi \& Newman \cite{nadakuditi2012}
locate the resulting \emph{detectability threshold} and show that spectral
modularity maximisation is optimal in the sense that no method succeeds where it
fails. For sparse graphs the non-backtracking operator \cite{krzakala2013} and
the Bethe Hessian \cite{saade2014} push that threshold to the information-theoretic
limit and are the correct comparators if we ever move to a sparse regime.

\paragraph{Correlation-specific null models.} The single most relevant reference
is MacMahon \& Garlaschelli \cite{macmahon2015}: applying network community
detection to correlation matrices is \emph{intrinsically biased}, because the
null model implicit in modularity (the configuration model) is inconsistent with
the statistics of correlation matrices. They redefine the modularity matrix
using a random-matrix-theory null, subtracting both the market mode and the
Marchenko--Pastur bulk, so that the residual contains only genuine group
structure. This is exactly the object Paper~3 needed and did not use --- and it
is the natural ``correct'' modularity matrix for our comparison. The classical
RMT results it rests on are Laloux et al.\ \cite{laloux1999} and Plerou et al.\
\cite{plerou1999}. The recent \emph{Physics Reports} review by Masuda, Boyd,
Garlaschelli \& Mucha \cite{masuda2025} surveys the whole
``beyond-thresholding'' agenda and is the best single framing citation for why
Paper~3's construction is the weak link.

\paragraph{Financial network structure, for context.} The filtering tradition
--- minimum spanning trees \cite{mantegna1999} and planar maximally filtered
graphs \cite{tumminello2005} --- is the alternative to thresholding. Temporal
community structure in markets was studied by Fenn et al.\ \cite{fenn2011}
using the multislice modularity of Mucha et al.\ \cite{mucha2010}; Paper~3 cites
that machinery and does not use it, which is the direct cause of its Louvain
instability problem. Geometric alternatives (Ollivier--Ricci curvature) were
proposed as fragility indicators by Sandhu, Georgiou \& Tannenbaum
\cite{sandhu2016} and are a good third baseline.

\subsection{The gap}

Putting the map together:

\begin{itemize}
\item The Laplacian spectrum has been used as a market indicator, in at least
three different forms ($\lambda_2$, the maximum spectral gap, Laplacian energy).
\item The modularity \emph{matrix} has a well-developed spectral theory, with
results --- structural eigenvalue counts, detectability thresholds,
anti-community eigenvalues --- that are exactly the sort of thing a market
indicator would want.
\item \textbf{Nobody has applied the modularity spectrum to markets.} Searches
across the finance-network literature return modularity used only as a scalar
$Q$ from a partition. No paper reports $\mu_1(\Bmat)$, $\mu_N(\Bmat)$, the count
of structural eigenvalues, or the spectral gap of $\Bmat$ as a time series for a
market.
\item No paper compares the two operators on common data with a common scoring
protocol, and the one theoretical fact that makes such a comparison sharp ---
Eq.~\eqref{eq:duality} --- appears not to have been used as an organising
principle.
\end{itemize}

We are unusually well-placed to close this: the pipeline, the 6{,}315-day
network sequence, the pre-registered crisis windows and the scoring code all
already exist, and the audit already tells us where the traps are.

\section{Part IV --- Three proposed directions}
\label{sec:directions}
% =====================================================================

They are ordered by how directly they answer your question. D1 is the core
comparative study; D2 is the theoretical contribution that makes D1 mean
something; D3 is the extension that uses the existing findings most aggressively.
D1 and D2 naturally combine into a single paper; D3 could be a second.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{D1 --- The operator comparison: duality, and what breaks it}

\textbf{Question.} $\lambda_2(\Lap)$ and $\mu_1(\Bmat)$ are affine duals on a
regular graph (Eq.~\ref{eq:duality}) and near-orthogonal on real market networks
($r = -0.17$). Decompose the residual. Which component of it --- degree
heterogeneity, fragmentation, or the market mode --- carries the market-stress
information, and which operator exposes it more directly?

\textbf{What we would build.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A single pipeline that, for each day, computes the full spectrum of a
\emph{family} of operators on the same network: $\Amat$, $\Lap$, the normalised
Laplacian $\mathcal{L} = \mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\Lap\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}$, $\Bmat$, the
normalised modularity matrix, and --- crucially --- the RMT-corrected
correlation modularity matrix of MacMahon \& Garlaschelli \cite{macmahon2015},
which needs no thresholding at all.
\item An exact decomposition of the duality residual $\bar d - [\lambda_2 +
\mu_1]$ into a degree-heterogeneity term and a connectivity term, validated
against the observed $9.49 \pm 4.63$.
\item A common scoring harness --- the Phase-0 pre-registered crisis windows,
trailing-$z$ detector, ROC-AUC, and out-of-sample chronological split --- so
that every operator is judged identically. This already exists and was used to
produce the numbers in Section~\ref{sec:prelim}.
\item Robustness over the parameters Paper~3 never tested: $\Delta t \in
\{20,30,60,120\}$, density $f$, universe size, and with/without market-mode
removal. This retires audit items A5 and A6 as a by-product.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why it is publishable.} It is not a horse race; it is a structural
result with a horse race attached. The claim ``these two indicators are the same
statistic on a regular graph and independent on market networks, and here is
exactly what the difference is'' is checkable, surprising, and useful to the
sizeable literature that picks one operator without argument.

\textbf{Main risk.} The decomposition may turn out to be dominated by
fragmentation, which is an artifact of fixed-density thresholding rather than a
property of markets. Mitigation: the largest-connected-component variant is
already computed (residual $11.11 \pm 5.31$, $r = -0.169$), so the finding
survives removing fragmentation --- but this needs to be established across
constructions, not just this one.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{D2 --- Detectability: is there community structure to detect at all?}

\textbf{Question.} Paper~3 estimates a $360\times360$ correlation matrix from 30
observations. The ratio $q = N/\Delta t = 12$ puts the Marchenko--Pastur band at
$[6.07, 19.93]$, and the whole matrix has rank at most 29. \emph{Under
random-matrix theory, how many eigenvalues of the daily correlation matrix
actually escape the noise bulk --- and therefore how many communities is it even
possible to detect?}

Preliminary answer, computed this week on all 6{,}315 days: excluding the market
mode, the mean number of eigenvalues above the Marchenko--Pastur upper edge is
$\mathbf{0.70}$, with a maximum of $3$. On a typical day there is \emph{less
than one} statistically distinguishable group mode. Louvain nonetheless returns
a median of 9 communities every day, and the paper builds a 25-year narrative on
their dynamics.

\textbf{What we would build.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \emph{detectability phase diagram} over $(\Delta t, N, f)$: for each
combination, where does the market sit relative to (a) the Marchenko--Pastur
edge and (b) the Nadakuditi--Newman spectral detectability threshold
\cite{nadakuditi2012} for the resulting network? This tells any practitioner in
this literature whether their parameter choice is inside or outside the region
where community structure is estimable.
\item A deterministic, algorithm-free replacement for $k(t)$: the count of
structural eigenvalues of $\Bmat$ (\cite{bolla2015,fasino2016}) and of
RMT-escaping modes of $\Cmat$, compared against Louvain's $k(t)$ and its
seed-to-seed spread. This directly retires the Louvain-instability problem (F3)
rather than measuring it.
\item A statement of how much of Paper~3's --- and the wider literature's ---
community dynamics survives once the estimable region is respected.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why it is publishable.} This is a methodological result the
thresholded-correlation-network literature needs and does not have; it is the
constructive version of the ``beyond thresholding'' argument \cite{masuda2025}.
It also converts our strongest replication finding --- that i.i.d.\ noise
reproduces 80\% of the market's modularity excess --- from a simulation into a
theorem-backed statement.

\textbf{Main risk.} The answer may be uninteresting in one direction: if
essentially \emph{no} configuration used in the literature is inside the
detectable region, the contribution is purely negative. That is still worth
publishing, but it is a different (and harder to place) paper. Mitigation: the
phase diagram will identify \emph{which} $(\Delta t, N)$ combinations are
usable, which is constructive.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{D3 --- The negative spectrum: anti-communities and the 2002 break}

\textbf{Question.} The Laplacian is blind to sign and the thresholded network
deletes every negative correlation. Paper~3's construction discards
$4{,}246{,}798$ pair-days with $\rho < -\tau$ --- strong anticorrelations
recorded identically to no relationship at all --- and that series
\emph{collapses to near zero from roughly 2003 onward and never recovers}, close
to the paper's own unexplained 2002 structural break, which it lists as future
work. The modularity matrix, unlike the Laplacian, has a meaningful negative
spectrum: $\mu_N(\Bmat)$ and its eigenvector identify anti-communities
\cite{fasino2018anti}. \emph{Does the negative spectrum recover the discarded
signal, and does it explain the 2002 break?}

Preliminary: $\mu_N(\Bmat)$ detects the pre-registered crises at ROC-AUC
$\mathbf{0.760}$ --- better than every Laplacian statistic we computed, and far
better than $Q$'s $0.538$.

\textbf{What we would build.} Signed correlation networks with no thresholding
step; the signed Laplacian and SPONGE-style generalised eigenproblems as
comparators; the negative modularity spectrum as the indicator; and a formal
change-point analysis of the 2002--2003 transition in the sign structure,
against candidate mechanisms (decimalisation in 2001, ETF and index-fund growth,
algorithmic trading).

\textbf{Why it is publishable.} It answers a question the original authors
explicitly left open, using a quantity they structurally could not see.

\textbf{Main risk.} $\mu_N(\Bmat)$ correlates $0.68$ with the market-mode
signal, so part of its $0.760$ is not new information (see
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}). The direction is only worth pursuing if the
\emph{residual} after projecting out the market mode still carries signal ---
which is a two-day check, and should be the gate before committing.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Recommendation}

\textbf{D1 + D2 as one project, D3 gated on a quick check.} D1 alone risks being
a horse race; D2 alone risks being a negative result; together they are a single
coherent claim: \emph{here is the correct operator to read market community
structure from, here is the region of parameter space where reading it is
meaningful at all, and here is what the field's two incumbent indicators are
actually measuring.} D3 is a good second paper and a bad first one.

\section{Part V --- Preliminary evidence}
\label{sec:prelim}
% =====================================================================

Computed 12 August 2026 on the existing Paper-3 network sequence: $N = 360$
stocks, 6{,}315 daily networks, 1987--2011. For each day, the full spectrum of
$\Lap$, of $\Bmat$, and of the raw correlation matrix $\Cmat$ was computed
(three $360\times360$ symmetric eigendecompositions per day). Every signal was
then scored through the \emph{identical} detector already used in the
replication: trailing 250-day $z$-score using only data strictly before the day
scored, ROC-AUC against the Phase-0 pre-registered \emph{sharp}-tier crisis
windows (base rate 2.68\%). The sign of each detector (spike vs.\ drop) was
chosen to favour it, which is generous and is noted as such.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Signal} & \textbf{Operator} & \textbf{AUC (sharp)} \\
\midrule
Spectral entropy of $\Cmat$ (effective rank) & correlation & \textbf{0.822} \\
$\lambda_1(\Cmat)/N$ (absorption ratio) & correlation & 0.812 \\
$\tau(t)$ (fixed-density threshold) & --- & 0.807 \\
$\mu_N(\Bmat)$ (anti-community edge) & \textbf{modularity} & \textbf{0.760} \\
Isolated-node count & Laplacian null space & 0.756 \\
$\sum_{\mu_i > 0}\mu_i$ (positive modularity mass) & \textbf{modularity} & 0.750 \\
$\lambda_N(\Lap)$ (largest Laplacian eigenvalue) & Laplacian & 0.731 \\
$\#\{\mu_i > 0\}$ (structural eigenvalue count) & \textbf{modularity} & 0.722 \\
$\lambda_2(\Lap)$ on the largest component & Laplacian & 0.652 \\
$\mu_1 - \mu_2$ (modularity spectral gap) & \textbf{modularity} & 0.636 \\
$\lambda_2(\Lap)$ (Fiedler value, whole graph) & Laplacian & 0.617 \\
$\mu_1(\Bmat)$ (leading modularity eigenvalue) & \textbf{modularity} & 0.607 \\
$\#$ modes above Marchenko--Pastur edge & correlation & 0.619 \\
$Q(t)$ (Louvain modularity --- the paper's signal) & partition scalar & 0.538 \\
$\max_k(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_k)$ (max Laplacian gap) & Laplacian & 0.526 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Readings.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The modularity matrix beats the modularity scalar decisively.}
$Q(t)$ scores $0.538$; three separate statistics of the matrix it is derived
from score $0.72$--$0.76$. The information is in the operator; collapsing it to
one number through a stochastic partition destroys most of it. \emph{This is the
direct empirical answer to your question, and it is a clean one.}
\item \textbf{The Laplacian statistics are weak on this construction.} The
Fiedler value reaches $0.617$, and $0.652$ once fragmentation is removed by
restricting to the largest component. Note that this is \emph{not} a refutation
of Paper~1: it uses a learned, regularised Laplacian on 7 stocks, not a
thresholded 360-node network. The maximum-spectral-gap indicator of Kang et al.\
\cite{kang2025} scores $0.526$ --- essentially chance --- but again on a
different construction from theirs (fixed density, not distance filtration), so
this is a caution to check, not a contradiction.
\item \textbf{Everything above $0.75$ is one signal.} $\tau(t)$, the absorption
ratio and the spectral entropy of $\Cmat$ are pairwise correlated at $|r| >
0.99$; the isolated-node count correlates $0.92$ with $\tau$. They are all
measuring market-mode dominance. This matters: the ``best'' indicators in the
table are not four discoveries, they are one, and it is the oldest one in the
finance literature \cite{kritzman2011,laloux1999}.
\item \textbf{The modularity family is a genuinely different axis.}
$\mu_1(\Bmat)$ correlates $0.025$ with the market-mode detector --- essentially
orthogonal --- and $0.761$ with Louvain $Q$ in levels. So $\mu_1(\Bmat)$ is a
deterministic proxy for what Louvain is trying to measure, without Louvain's
$2\times$-the-signal seed jitter.
\item \textbf{But orthogonality did not translate into out-of-sample gain.}
Fitting a logistic detector on a pre-2002 training period and testing post-2002
(27 sharp-crisis days in the test set): market mode alone $0.730$; adding
$\mu_1(\Bmat)$ $0.629$; adding $Q$ $0.699$; adding $\mu_N(\Bmat)$ $0.719$.
In-sample all of these \emph{improve} on the market mode ($0.812 \to
0.822$--$0.831$), which is exactly the pattern you would expect from
overfitting. \textbf{With 27 out-of-sample events, none of this is conclusive in
either direction} --- it is a warning that the incremental-value claim needs a
much larger event set (more markets, more assets, longer history) before it can
be made.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The detectability number.} Excluding the market mode, the mean count
of correlation eigenvalues above the Marchenko--Pastur edge is $0.70$ (max 3,
often 0) at $\Delta t = 30$, $N = 360$. This is the single most consequential
preliminary number in this document: it says that at the window length used
throughout this literature, the daily correlation matrix contains
\emph{essentially no statistically resolvable group structure}, while the
methods applied to it confidently report nine communities a day.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}\sloppy Everything in this section is in
\texttt{reports/probe/}: three scripts (\texttt{spectral\_probe.py},
\texttt{duality.py}, \texttt{incremental.py}), the per-day spectra
(\texttt{spectral\_probe.parquet}, 6{,}315 rows $\times$ 15 columns) and the AUC
tables as JSON. They import the replication's own \texttt{crisis\_dates},
\texttt{construct} and scoring code unchanged --- the same pre-registered
windows, the same 250-day trailing $z$-score --- so every number above is
directly comparable to the replication's own. As a check on that claim, the
probe's $Q(t)$ and $\tau(t)$ AUCs reproduce the replication's stored Phase-8
values ($0.5380684370$ and $0.8071316467$) to machine precision, so the new
spectral rows sit on exactly the same scoring protocol as the published ones. Runtime is about 6 minutes for the
full spectral pass on one core.\par\fussy
